STRATEGY B RETROSPECTIVE — AUDIO AUGMENTATION FOR CLASS-IMBALANCE
==================================================================
Status:  Closed. Failure


1. GOAL
-------
Reduce the 24.5x maximum-to-minimum class imbalance in the segment-level
training set, using audio-domain augmentation, in the hope of lifting
macro F1 on rare classes (light_switch, bell_ringing, etc.).

Strategy B was the audio-augmentation arm. Strategy A is the no-augmentation
control, with class-weighted losses / sample weights doing all the
imbalance lifting.


2. WHAT WAS BUILT
-----------------
- Donor-event extraction: clean segments of rare-class events identified
  via a high-pure-fraction filter, then grouped into events (not segments)
  to avoid duplicating augmentation across the 50%-overlapping 1 s windows.

- Per-class augmentation menu, tuned to event duration:
  * Sustained events (bell_ringing, coffee_machine, toilet_flushing):
    pitch shift, time stretch, gain, AddGaussianSNR.
  * Transient events (light_switch): no time stretch (smears the impulse),
    pitch shift / gain only.
  * PolarityInversion: free for spectrogram-input models; kept for
    waveform-input models.

- Budget rule: synthetic-to-real ratio capped at 3x per class, to avoid
  synthetic dilution.

- Re-extraction of all 56 aggregated feature keys with the same features.py
  used on real audio, preserving schema parity.

Result of the pipeline (counts only, not performance):
  * 5,205 new audio files generated
  * 24,224 cumulative class-positive segments added across the 7 rare classes
  * Imbalance max:min ratio: 24.5x -> 7.3x (segment counts)
  * Six of seven rare classes within +/-10% of the dataset median (9,436)
  * light_switch hit the 3x cap (1,047 -> 3,515)


3. WHAT WAS FOUND (TEST MACRO F1, A vs B)
-----------------------------------------
                A          B          delta (B-A)    verdict
  LogReg     0.4723     0.4749       +0.003          noise
  Random Forest  0.5040 0.4982       -0.006          noise
  XGBoost    0.5605     0.5564       -0.004          noise
  MLP v2     0.5495     0.5534       +0.004          noise

Delta range across four model families: [-0.006, +0.004]. Smaller than the
seed-variance noise floor for any of these models. No family showed a
significant lift from augmentation.

Per-class deltas on the augmentation-targeted rare classes (XGBoost A vs B):
  bell_ringing       3.40x aug   -0.052   regression (largest)
  coffee_machine     1.36x aug   -0.004   noise
  door_open_close    1.13x aug   +0.004   noise
  light_switch       3.36x aug   -0.013   regression
  toilet_flushing    1.47x aug   +0.004   noise
  wardrobe_drawer    1.86x aug   -0.012   noise
  window_open_close  2.78x aug   +0.036   small lift
  ----------------------------------------------------------
  Mean over 7 classes:           -0.005   net regression

The two classes pushed hardest (3.4x and 3.36x) both regressed. The only
clear lift came from window_open_close on XGBoost. The signal is incoherent.


4. WHY IT FAILED (MECHANISM)
----------------------------
The 960-dimensional feature vector is built from four time aggregations
(mean / std / min / max) of 14 base feature groups over each 1 s segment.
That aggregation is a low-pass filter on time-domain perturbations.

A pitch-shifted bell still produces nearly the same MFCC mean as the
original bell, because the shift moves spectral energy by a fraction of a
mel bin and averaging smooths the shift out. Time-stretching changes
duration, which the aggregations also collapse. AddGaussianSNR raises the
noise floor, but the mean and std absorb it.

Consequence: the augmented feature vectors are statistically very close to
the original ones, so they do not add new information to the training
manifold. They behave like duplicated samples with mild jitter --
equivalent to class-weighted training, which Strategy A already does.

This is consistent across linear (LogReg), bagged-tree (RF), boosted-tree
(XGB), and neural (MLP) inductive biases. The bottleneck is the feature
representation, not the classifier.


5. DECISION
-----------
Strategy B is closed. No further training runs on augmented features.

The audio_aug/ files and the augmentation/ pipeline are kept on disk as
read-only history for the report and for any future spectrogram-input model
that bypasses the aggregation step. They are not the path forward for
the aggregated-feature pipeline.


6. WHAT TO DO INSTEAD
---------------------
Move from aggregated features to raw mel-spectrograms, where pitch shifts
and time stretches are visible perturbations rather than averaged-out
jitter. The next models in this project (CNN, LSTM, AST) operate on raw
mel-spectrograms and can in principle exploit augmentation -- but the
correct test of augmentation on those models is a separate experiment and
not in scope here.

Within the aggregated-feature pipeline, all imbalance lifting comes from:
  * class_weight='balanced' for sklearn classifiers
  * sqrt(inverse_freq)-clipped pos_weight for BCEWithLogitsLoss
  * per-class threshold tuning on the validation set
which together do the job that augmentation was supposed to do, and at
much lower compute cost.


7. WHY THIS IS A USEFUL NEGATIVE RESULT
---------------------------------------
The result is mechanistic, not accidental: aggregated features cannot
exploit audio-domain augmentation. This is testable, reproducible across
four model families, and explains why feature-domain SMOTE (the obvious
alternative) would also be marginal -- both move on the manifold of
*averages*, not the manifold of *real recordings*.

For the final report, Strategy B is presented as a controlled negative
experiment that motivates the move to spectrogram-input models. Sample
size of trained models supporting the conclusion: 4 (LogReg, RF, XGB, MLP).


8. NUMBERS FOR THE REPORT (PRE-WRITTEN ANCHORS)
-----------------------------------------------
"Audio augmentation produced 5,205 new files contributing 24,224 class-
positive segments. The maximum-to-minimum segment-count ratio fell from
24.5x to 7.3x. On test macro F1, however, the augmentation delta (B - A)
across four model families (LogReg, RF, XGB, MLP) lay in [-0.006, +0.004],
within seed-variance noise. The two classes pushed hardest by the
augmentation budget (bell_ringing 3.4x and light_switch 3.36x) showed

vectors lie close to their originals on the aggregated manifold and add
little new information."
